# Verification

## The Iron Law

**You MUST NOT claim work is complete, fixed, passing, working, or ready without fresh verification evidence produced in the current message.**

"Fresh" means the verification command ran after your last change to the code it verifies; a run from a previous message or before your latest edit does not count. "Evidence" means the command's actual output: exit code, pass/fail counts, build status. Your belief that it would pass is not evidence.

## Scope

The rule covers every statement that asserts or implies success: direct claims ("tests pass", "fixed", "done", "ready"), satisfaction language ("Great!", "All good"), hedged claims ("should work now", "probably fine"), and implicit claims (moving on, committing, opening or updating a pull request, handing back, accepting a subagent's report, where you verify the diff rather than the claim). Spirit over letter: if the reader could take your message to mean the work is done, the rule applies, and rewording is not an escape.

The `work-verification` skill is the executable gate.
